About StackSpan

By StackSpan Editors · Updated September 2026

StackSpan is a gear-and-habits site for people who want to keep moving for the long haul without buying every new thing. It covers wider footwear, simple foot care, short mobility routines, garage-style training, and the long history of physical culture.

Why it exists

StackSpan is written for a familiar kind of reader: past forty, at a desk most weekdays, and trying to make the most of the rest of life. For some that means surfing or skating. For others it’s sandbag carries in the garage, or a pair of barefoot shoes for the walk to lunch. That reader has also grown tired of neomania, the pull toward whatever is newest: the latest supplement, the miracle gadget, the protocol nobody will mention next year.

The biologist E.O. Wilson put the modern problem this way: humans have Paleolithic emotions, medieval institutions, and godlike technology. StackSpan bets on the Paleolithic side of that equation, on purpose. It looks for habits and tools that have lasted: walking, carrying, play, plain food, and shoes shaped like feet.

The idea behind that bet is the Lindy effect, popularized by Nassim Nicholas Taleb: something that has stayed useful for a long time is a reasonable bet to stay useful a while longer. A shoe shaped like a foot was around long before this year’s cushioning trend, and it will likely be around after the next one.

Some of the best teachers are the old ones. When in doubt, blame it on Jack LaLanne. The physical culture elders hub collects strongmen, wrestlers, and Muscle Beach regulars who kept training late in life. It’s there as history and inspiration, with survivorship bias labeled.

Who writes it

StackSpan Editors is the site’s house byline. StackSpan is independent. No brand owns it or sponsors it. Pages are researched and drafted with help from software tools, including AI, and a human editor reviews every page before it goes live. StackSpan doesn’t publish personal names or photos. The pages should earn trust by being useful.

What StackSpan is not

It’s not a clinic. Nothing here is medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. If something hurts sharply, gets worse, or changes how you walk, see a clinician.

How picks are made

  • Fit and function first: toe-box shape, stack height, drop, flexibility, sizing notes, and return terms.
  • Researched unless marked: picks are shortlisted from specs, listings, and return terms unless a page says a pick is hands-on. The hands-on tag is added only after real wear time.
  • Price bands, not live prices: retailer prices change, so StackSpan lists bands (for example, under $60) and links out for today’s price.
  • No star ratings or scores that suggest lab testing.
  • Slow transitions, not transformations: no promises about pain, conditions, weight, or lifespan.
  • Dated and correctable: pages carry an “updated” date, and errors sent to StackSpan get fixed.
  • No paid placement: brands don’t pay to be listed and don’t review copy.

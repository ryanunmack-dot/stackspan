Garage sandbag primer

A short shopping and habit guide for soft-style sandbags in a garage or driveway — strength practice language, not a program, medical protocol, or longevity cure.

BLUF: Start with a fillable soft bag you can load lightly, learn clean pickups and carries before heavy throws, and progress only when form feels boring. We wire existing WOLF Tactical /go links (100 lb and 150 lb capacity styles) — check live Amazon for fill type, handles, and current price.
Safety: Clear the floor. Wear shoes with grip. Keep kids/pets out of the work area. Start light — a half-filled bag is fine. Stop if something feels sharp or wrong. Soft bags can shift mid-rep — brace and control the load. Not medical advice; get coaching if you are new to loaded carries or lifts.

Starter habit (conservative)

  1. Warm ankles / hips lightly — optional foot-forward warm-up
  2. Fill lighter than you think; practice deadlift-style pickups to a stand
  3. 2–4 short carries (down the driveway and back) with clean posture
  4. Optional: a few controlled clean-to-shoulder or bear-hug holds — stop early
  5. Leave the bag partially filled until sessions feel boring, then add sand

Strength habit language only — not a periodized program or medical prescription.

Footwear note

Use shoes with grip on concrete. Wider / flexible trainers are fine for many garage days — see the shoes hub — but prioritize traction and stability over fashion claims.

FAQ

100 or 150 lb capacity?

~100 lb if you want a smaller footprint and lighter max. ~150 lb if you want one bag with more fill headroom later. Capacity is not day-one load — start light either way.

Do I fill it completely on day one?

No. Leave headroom empty until pickups and short carries feel boring. Soft bags shift mid-rep — brace and control the load.

Bunion satire · Start here first

Narrow, pointy shoes can crowd the toes and feel worse on a bunion.

By StackSpan Editors

Start here: Shoes that taper where the toes need space can press on a bunion and make the day less comfortable. A wide toe box and a few simple foot-care tools are small changes that many people find more comfortable. Surgery is a decision for you and a clinician.

Pointy toe box compared with a foot-shaped toe box.

Fashion spent a century favoring shoes with a sharp, narrow prow. Feet are wider at the toes than most of those shoes, and many people find tapered shoes crowd the forefoot. Footwear is about fit and comfort; medical options are a separate conversation with a clinician.

This page is not medical advice. It is a calm consumer guide to fit, toe room, and everyday habits.

What many people try first

  • Shoes that do not squeeze the big toe toward the second toe
  • Time barefoot or in wide, flexible soles when it is safe
  • Gentle massage or rolling during low-load parts of the day
  • See a clinician if pain is sharp, worsening, or changes how you walk, or if redness or numbness appears

Worth trying for everyday comfort

Prioritize wider shoes first. Foot tools and spacers are optional add-ons — not a substitute for room in the toe box. Not medical advice.
